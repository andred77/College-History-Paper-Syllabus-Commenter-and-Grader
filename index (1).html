Business Case

The Problem This Prototype Solves

The Problem

The problem that my prototype solves is for students to improve their essays based off of the syllabus and increase their grade. Students have this problem because the syllabus can be vague or hard to interpret when writing an essay. This affects teachers too as they can get overwhelmed helping out many students. How this is dealt with today is by students either turning in what they think is similar, going to a writing center, or going to the teacher for help. This is not enough as the student is reliant on the schedule and how helpful the teacher and tutoring center are, plus the tutoring center has a higher chance of interpreting the syllabus differently from how the teacher intended.

The User

My target user is second year history majors who are taking multiple history classes and have multiple writing assignments each week. What their experience was before is going back and forth between different syllabi while writing their essay and either checking in with a certain teacher or tutor if their availabilities lined up. Their experience will be improved and time will be saved. They can run their draft through the prototype and it will give a score based off the syllabus and give suggestions on how to improve the essay.

Why Should This Prototype Exist

AI is the right fit for this problem because it can give a score and specific feedback to improve the essay. This requires looking over the syllabus on what is required and running through the essay and grading and commenting on it. A student does not know where their essay will fall grade wise and what they need to do to improve it, and my prototype is the solution.

✦ ✦ ✦

The Biggest Risk

One way my prototype could fail is if a science student uses it to help with their research paper. Since my model is specifically intended for history majors with multiple papers, the model is trained on those papers and the format of a research paper is different, and this could lead to a lower grade or comments that tell the user to use more outside sources.

Course Concepts

Data Dependency: How data-dependency shows up in my prototype is that the model is dependent on graded and commented college history papers. Without the comments on the papers the model will not know how to accurately give a score or know what to comment.

Long Tail Failure: How the long tail failure shows up in my model is that the model will need a lot of graded and commented essays and the syllabus. This can be hard to come by as those three are protected by the school's privacy policy.

AI Factory

How It Works

The system follows a structured AI pipeline that takes in the syllabus and a student's draft essay, compares it against example graded papers, and produces a grade with specific feedback.

Data

The syllabus for the class, the drafted essay, and previous essays from different students that are already graded and commented on, so the model can learn how to grade and give suggestions.

Model

Machine learning analyzes the essay and produces a score. Generative AI runs through the essay and comments on what should change based on the syllabus criteria.

Prediction

The system predicts a grade and generates suggestions based off of the syllabus and previously graded assignments used as calibration examples.

Decision

The model delivers a letter grade, a score out of 100, category-level scores, per-category feedback, and a ranked list of the top improvements to make.

Value

Saved time, better grades, less stress, ability to focus more on bigger assignments, and a clear path to improving writing skills by understanding exactly what a professor expects.
